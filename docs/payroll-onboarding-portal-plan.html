Payroll Onboarding Portal

A plan for a click-through card deck that teaches new employers what the payroll setup call covers, and lets confident clients finish without waiting for a call.

For: Brendan (Payroll) and Kevin Covers: layout, interactions and content. Branding comes later. Sources: First Call Step by Step, Call alternative (Staffology), setup meeting of 21 Sept 2026

Items marked ⚑ Confirm need Brendan to confirm the current process or figure.

1 · Summary

What we're building

A friendly deck of cards covering who's who, the monthly routine, the documents employers get, HMRC, and their responsibilities. It is not a test. It's split into short sections the employer can come back to at any time, for example opening the payslip section with their first payslip in front of them.

At the end they choose "I'm confident, start my payroll" or "I'd like a call". The call stays as the safety net. The portal is an alternative for confident clients, not a replacement for everyone.

Goals from the meeting

GoalWhat it means for the design
Fewer setup calls. Even 30–35% of clients skipping the call is a win.A clear choice at the end. Confident clients finish without waiting for an appointment.
Keep the human feel, CILNI's biggest selling pointWarm, plain English. Brendan's voice narrates each card. A "you're not alone" message. A call is always one tap away.
People take in the key points instead of racing to the tick boxShort cards with one idea each. Key facts large and in colour. Light interactions and recap cards.
No quizzes, because they feel like a testCalculators, tap-to-reveal and "Got it" recaps. Never right or wrong answers.
Persistent, so people can come backSaved progress, a section hub, and every section stays open after they finish.
Covers CILNI on liabilityRecord which cards were viewed, plus a timestamped acknowledgement at the end.
Works on a phone or tabletDesigned for mobile first. Big buttons, swipe to go forward or back, landscape works.

Who it's for

The person who will be the registered employer. That's often a parent or family member managing a direct payment for the service user. Technical ability varies hugely: some clients are very confident, others are proud just to get a password in. Write for a stressed reader who has just been through a long process with the Trust.

3 · Screens & cards

The three main screens

Rough layouts only. Styling and branding come later.

After they finish, the hub becomes their reference page. The quick links matter most then: "I've just got my first payslip, what does it mean?"

Rules for every card

One idea per cardA heading and 1–3 short sentences, about 40 words at most.
Facts and figures stand outNumbers, dates and do/don'ts are larger and in colour: 7 days, 13 characters, 5.6 weeks.
"Tell me more"An expander for the detail Brendan adds on the call, so the card itself stays short.
Brendan's narrationOptional audio with a transcript. His tone carries emphasis and reassurance text can't, especially employer vs employee.
"I'd like help with this"Flags the card. Flagged topics go with any call request, so Brendan knows what someone struggled with.
Jargon is tappableUnderlined terms open a plain-English definition, for example nominated representative or P30.

Every card, section by section

Draft wording in the portal's voice: plain English, second person, warm. About 55–60 cards in total.

0 · Welcome

3 cards

  • 0.2You're not aloneSetting up as an employer can feel like a lot, especially after everything with the Trust. Many of our team have been through direct payments themselves, or with family. Every situation is a bit different, but we'll always do our best to help.

1 · Who's who

6 cards · ~3 min

Most submission-form mistakes come from getting these roles wrong, so this section comes first.

  • 1.1You are the employerThe employer is the person who agreed with CILNI's Advice Team to take on this role. HMRC will register you and write to you. Tell me more: this isn't always the person who receives care. If you manage the account for a family member, it's usually you.
  • 1.2The employeeYour employee is the person you pay to provide support, such as a personal assistant. The employer and the employee can never be the same person.
  • 1.3The Trust funds you. We run the payroll.Your Health & Social Care Trust provides the funding. CILNI is your payroll provider. We don't hold your money, can't see your bank account, and can't change your funding.
  • 1.4What you tell us, we report ⚑ ConfirmAs a payroll bureau, we're legally required to report any hours and pay you tell us about to HMRC. So only tell us about work paid for from your direct payment. A gift to a friend or family member isn't payroll. If you're unsure whether something counts, ask us before you send it in. Wording to agree with Brendan and Kevin. This replaces the tactful "have a think before you tell us" line from the call.
  • 1.5Nominated representativeSomeone you choose to deal with CILNI on your behalf, using your authority. You can remove them at any time. Only you or your nominated representative can make changes. Social workers can't.
  • 1.6RecapYou're the employer · the Trust funds and we run payroll · we must report what you tell us. Got it

2 · Your staff and their pay

7 cards · ~4 min
  • 2.1Check your detailsBefore we start, we'll confirm each employee's start date, hours and rate of pay. These often change after the Advice Team first sees them, so tell us if anything is different.
  • 2.2Set hours or variable hours? ⚑ ConfirmSide by side. Monthly (set hours): the same hours every month, predictable. Variable (zero hours): hours change month to month. Tell me more: pros and cons of each. Brendan to supply the points he gives on the call.
  • 2.3Your pay rate ⚑ ConfirmYour rate is either set by a budget from our team, or chosen by you, as long as it's at or above the National Minimum Wage. Caution: if you choose your own rate, our support is limited if it leads to a surplus or shortfall in funding. Show the current minimum wage figure.
  • 2.4Your pay date (try it)Your pay date is one calendar month after your first employee starts. Calculator: enter the start date → Your first pay date is 17 June 2026, then the 17th of every month.
  • 2.5Payments for earlier workIf you ask us to pay for work done before payroll started, we can, but it goes through payroll and it will be taxed.
  • 2.6CILNI's fees ⚑ Confirm"Give details about CILNI payments" is on the call checklist. Brendan to supply the content.
  • 2.7RecapGot it

3 · Your monthly routine

7 cards · ~4 min

The heart of the portal. Brendan's end-of-call summary: we email you, you check it, we send documents, you pay the employee and HMRC, and you move on with your month.

  • 3.1The month in 4 stepsCycle diagram: ① We email you → ② You check the hours → ③ We send your documents → ④ You pay your employee and HMRC.
  • 3.2① Our email: 7 days before pay day ⚑ ConfirmEach month, 7 days before your pay date, we'll email asking you to confirm your employees' hours. The written guide says always reply. Brendan's call summary says "no changes? you can ignore it". Which is correct?
  • 3.3② The three types of hoursStandard hours: based on your weekly funding from the Trust, and can be shared across employees. Annual leave: paid time off, and we keep a running total. Extra hours: for example covering someone's holiday, shown separately on the payslip.
  • 3.4Reply to payroll@cilni.orgSend your hours back as soon as you can. We need at least 1 full working day to prepare your documents. In busy periods, please allow up to your pay date.
  • 3.5③ What arrivesPayslips · summary for the Trust · P30 (HMRC payment). Each is covered in section 4.
  • 3.6④ Pay dayPay your employee the net pay shown at the bottom of their payslip, and pay HMRC the amount on your P30, ideally on the same day.
  • 3.7RecapGot it

4 · Your documents

10 cards · ~5 min
  • 4.1Opening your payslips 🔒Payslips are password-protected. Pick a password you're happy to share with your employee.
  • 4.2Password trouble?Tips: passwords are case-sensitive · watch for extra spaces · try copying and pasting it · still stuck? Tap "I'd like help" and we'll check it with you. This is the biggest frustration on calls, so it gets its own card and, later, a 60-second video.
  • 4.3Reading a payslip (annotated)Sample payslip with hotspots: ① gross pay ② tax code ③ income tax ④ National Insurance ⑤ pension ⑥ student loan, if any ⑦ net pay, the amount you pay.
  • 4.4Why are there deductions? ⚑ ConfirmTell me more cards on tax code, NI and pension in plain English, based on Brendan's explanations from the call.
  • 4.5Check it, then send it ⚑ ConfirmIf the payslip looks right, send it to your employee with their pay. If something's wrong, tell us and we'll fix it before you pay. Employees can also receive payslips directly. Is this still offered?
  • 4.6The summary for the Trust (annotated)A gross-to-net summary of each month's payroll. Keep it for your records. The Trust may ask for it quarterly, along with your bank statements.
  • 4.7Why the Trust wants bank statements ⚑ ConfirmThey check your spending matches your payroll. Tell me more: not every Trust asks regularly. Belfast Trust is the one most likely to.
  • 4.8Your bufferThe Trust pays an advance of 5–8 weeks of funding, so there should always be money in the account on pay day.
  • 4.9The P30 (annotated)Shows one total owed to HMRC for all your employees: income tax, NI and student loan combined. You don't need to work anything out.
  • 4.10RecapGot it

5 · HMRC

7 cards · ~4 min

A real case: a client didn't read the written guide, paid nothing, and was then angry about HMRC letters. This section has to stand out.

  • 5.1We register you with HMRCOnce you've confirmed your details are right, we register you as an employer with HMRC. It's the tax for your employees, not your own tax bill.
  • 5.2⚠ Don't pay HMRC yetUntil your HMRC references arrive, don't pay HMRC. You can still pay your employees, and the payslip figures are correct. Big warning styling.
  • 5.3Your 13-character reference ⚑ ConfirmIn about 2 weeks we'll email your HMRC bank details and your 13-character reference. It never changes. Confirm the timescale.
  • 5.4Set up HMRC as a payeeAdd HMRC as a payee in your online banking with your 13-character reference. Then each month, just pay that payee. Other options: cheque or phone. Tell me more: step by step for common banks.
  • 5.5Why the dates don't match ⚑ ConfirmHMRC's months run from the 6th to the 5th, so the tax period on your P30 won't always match your pay date. That's normal. Check this matches how Brendan explains it.
  • 5.6What would you do? (tap to reveal)"You get an HMRC letter asking for money." Already paid? Contact us and we'll help challenge it. Not paid? Pay the amount due. A little interest may be added.
  • 5.7RecapGot it

6 · Your responsibilities as an employer

10 cards · ~5 min
  • 6.1Don't pay cashAlways pay by bank transfer or cheque. Cash leaves no record, and if an employee disputes their pay you'd have no proof.
  • 6.2Sickness ⚑ ConfirmIf your employee is off sick, tell us when you send in the hours. They may be entitled to Statutory Sick Pay.
  • 6.3Maternity, paternity and other statutory pay ⚑ ConfirmTell us as early as possible. We'll work out what's due.
  • 6.4Holidays: 5.6 weeks (try it) ⚑ ConfirmEmployees are entitled to 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year, including bank holidays. Calculator: days worked per week → yearly entitlement. Confirm how CILNI handles part-time and variable hours.
  • 6.5Holiday year and carry-over ⚑ ConfirmYour holiday year runs from ___. Up to 1.6 weeks can be carried over. Cover: record it as extra hours for whoever covers.
  • 6.6Employer's liability insurance ⚑ ConfirmA short card on what it is and that it's required. Brendan only covers this if asked. Should it be a "Tell me more" instead?
  • 6.7Workplace pension ⚑ ConfirmEmployees who meet the age and earnings thresholds must be automatically enrolled. We'll tell you when someone goes in and what to do. Current thresholds and CILNI's process.
  • 6.8Letters from The Pensions RegulatorEvery 3 years you'll get a letter about a declaration or re-declaration. We get a copy and deal with it, so just file it away.
  • 6.9Your account, your sayOur contract is with you. Changes must come from you or your nominated representative.
  • 6.10RecapGot it

5 · Engagement

Keeping people engaged without a quiz

  • Short cards with one idea each, and the key number jumps out.
  • Brendan's voice on every card for tone, emphasis and reassurance.
  • "Try it" calculators for pay date and holiday. They make the rule personal, and nothing is submitted, so nothing can go wrong.
  • Annotated sample documents. Tapping around a sample payslip is interactive and useful.
  • "What would you do?" scenarios with the answer revealed, never scored.
  • A recap at the end of each section with a "Got it" button. The hub fills up as they go.
  • Saved progress, so they can stop without losing anything.
  • Welcome video now, 60–90 second clips later (for example "Opening your payslip", "Setting up HMRC as a payee"), placed on the matching cards.

Chris is also researching learning and retention principles. Anything useful slots in here.

6 · Two versions

Should it also replace the submission form?

The meeting left this open, so the plan covers both.

Recommended first

Version A: Learn only

What it is
The card deck above. The submission form stays separate, as now.
Pros
Simple. No new data errors. Quick to build and test.
Cons
Clients still do two things: the form and the portal.
Needs
Nothing extra.

Version B: Learn and submit

What it is
"A little explanation, a little information-gathering." Each data card sits right after the card that explains it. For example, employer details come straight after card 1.1 You are the employer.
Pros
One journey instead of two forms. Data is collected at the moment it's understood. The client could be live at the end.
Cons
Every field is a chance for a panicked mistake. Needs a staff review step, validation, and a proper web app rather than Canva.
Needs
The safeguards below and a draft → approve workflow.

Recommendation: build and pilot Version A first. Structure the cards so Version B's data cards can be added later without rewriting content.

Safeguards Version B would need

Based on the common submission-form errors Brendan described.

  • Employer and employee can't be the same person. Flag it if the same name appears twice.
  • Ask "Are you filling this in for someone else?" first, so the service user's name doesn't end up in the employer fields by mistake.
  • Ask for the direct payment account, and warn if the bank details look like the employee's.
  • "Are the funds available in your direct payment account?" gets a follow-up if the answer is No, and a gentle check if it looks inconsistent.
  • Address and postcode lookup to cut down typos.
  • Every submission arrives as a Draft. Payroll can edit and correct it, then Approve. No "reject and start again": re-doing a 30-minute journey would lose people.

8 · Phases

Suggested order of work

  1. Content sign-offBrendan reviews the card content and answers the ⚑ items. Agree wording for the tricky cards: 1.4 "we must report" and W.4 acknowledgement.
  2. Clickable prototype (Version A)Placeholder illustrations, no narration. Test with Brendan, Kevin and a couple of friendly clients.
  3. PilotSend the link to a handful of new employers before their normal setup call. Measure how much shorter the call gets and what people flag.
  4. Narration and welcome videoThen offer the "confident, no call" path for real.
  5. Branding and visual design
  6. Web appProgress saved across devices and a staff dashboard. Optionally Version B.

How we'll know it's working

  • The share of clients choosing "no call"
  • Time to complete
  • The most-flagged cards, and whether they need rewriting
  • HMRC and payslip-password queries in the first 3 months, compared with clients who had a call
